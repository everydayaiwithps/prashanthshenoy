Research

AI literacy for educators

Teachers are expected to use generative AI before anyone has agreed on what AI literacy for educators should contain, or how a teacher would show they can judge an AI system's output. My doctoral research, Advancing AI Literacy for Educators: Framework Synthesis, Governance, and Cross-National Empirical Validation (defence planned for November 2027, supervisor Associate Professor Mirka Saarela), works through that problem in four steps.

An umbrella review of AI literacy frameworks (P2) found fragmented foundations, almost no standardised assessment and little attention to explainability. Those gaps shaped the GenAI-TPD framework (P1): five competency domains across four developmental stages, with Critical Evaluation as a distinct competency because generative AI produces fluent, confident and sometimes wrong output. A policy mapping of India's national initiatives against UNESCO and OECD standards (P3) and an analysis of the EU AI Act (P4) place the framework in its governance context. Field studies with teachers in Bengaluru (P5, P6, P10) measure adoption intention and readiness.

The next step moves from self-report to performance. A planned 2027 study with about 300 teachers at IIT Bombay will use a seeded-error instrument to test whether educators can actually detect and evaluate errors in AI-generated content.

Twelve research outputs to date, ten as first author. Research carried out in association with Research Council of Finland project no. 356314.

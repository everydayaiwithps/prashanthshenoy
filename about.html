About

Researching how teachers learn to work with AI

I am an AI in Education (AIED) researcher in the Faculty of Information Technology at the University of Jyväskylä, Finland, and a member of the HUMBLE research group (Human and Machine-Based Intelligence in Learning). My research is supported by the Research Council of Finland.

My work answers a question every school system now faces. Generative AI arrived in classrooms faster than any technology before it, and teachers were expected to adapt with almost no preparation. What do teachers actually need to know, why do some adopt AI while others quietly resist, and what kind of professional development changes practice rather than just raising awareness?

I approach this at three levels. At the level of evidence, I synthesise what the field already knows, through an umbrella review of 28 systematic reviews and a meta-analysis of teacher AI adoption covering nearly 16,000 teachers in 19 countries. At the level of the classroom, I design and test interventions with teachers in India and study teachers in Finland, so that frameworks are checked against real staffrooms. At the level of the system, I analyse policy and governance: how national AI literacy initiatives measure up to international standards, and how AI tools in education should explain themselves.

I work across India and Finland on purpose. One is a vast, centrally guided system running some of the world's largest AI-first education initiatives. The other is a high-trust, teacher-autonomous system. Comparing them shows what is universal about teacher AI literacy and what depends on context.

My MS in Machine Learning from the University of Arizona means I study AI in education from both sides: how the models work, and how teachers and learners experience them. I also co-facilitate a Master's-level Digital Competencies module with Universidade Aberta, Portugal.

I do not believe research should stay in journals. Most of what I know about teachers and AI I learned in staffrooms, so I spend part of every year in them. I work with whole school staff, usually in a 90-minute hands-on session or a full day, and the content is the evidence itself rather than a tour of tools. Each year I also run a free programme of AI literacy workshops for selected schools in India.

What a school gets out of that is specific. Staff leave able to judge AI output rather than just produce it, leaders get a reading of where their teachers actually stand before they spend anything on training, and the school can see what moved afterwards. If that is a conversation worth having at your institution, in person or online, write to me.

I work closely with Dr. Mirka Saarela, my co-author on almost all of this research, and with Dr. Ville Heilala.

The question is no longer whether teachers use AI. It is whether they use it with judgement.

Read the rest
His published work includes an umbrella review of AI literacy frameworks for educators (HICSS 59), the GenAI-TPD framework for integrating generative AI into teacher professional development (Discover Education), and a meta-analysis of teacher AI adoption covering nearly 16,000 teachers in 19 countries (Artificial Intelligence Review). He also studies AI governance in education, from India's national AI literacy policy to runtime explainability, and the pedagogy of AI tutors. He takes this evidence into schools through whole-staff AI literacy workshops and full-day programmes for teachers and leaders, in person and online. He works closely with Dr. Mirka Saarela, and his research is supported by the Research Council of Finland.

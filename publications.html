Explain Published

Explaining teacher adoption of AI through the Technology Acceptance Model: a meta-analysis and systematic review

Shenoy, P. & Saarela, M. Artificial Intelligence Review, Springer, 2026. DOI: 10.1007/s10462-026-11736-x

The largest synthesis of its kind on why teachers adopt AI, and what the standard model leaves out.

What we found
What we found
Across 38 studies covering nearly 16,000 teachers in 19 countries, the core Technology Acceptance Model relationships (usefulness, ease of use and intention to use) hold strongly, but results vary enormously by context. We propose TAM-I, an extension that adds teachers' professional identity and epistemic beliefs.
Why it matters
Leaders cannot rely on "AI is useful" messaging. Adoption depends on context and on what AI means for teachers' sense of their profession.

A critical review and actionable framework for integrating generative AI into teacher professional development

Shenoy, P. & Saarela, M. Discover Education, Springer Nature, 2026. DOI: 10.1007/s44217-026-01579-7

The GenAI-TPD framework: a staged roadmap for teacher professional development in generative AI.

What we found
What we found
Synthesising 20 peer-reviewed studies, we propose an affordance-based model with five competency domains, four developmental stages and four affordance categories (functional, cognitive, social, reflective), and identify theoretical gaps in current approaches.
Why it matters
It replaces one-off tool training with a progression that schools can plan, sequence and assess. Other researchers are already applying it, including in secondary language classrooms in China.

AI literacy frameworks for educators: an umbrella review

Shenoy, P. & Saarela, M. Proceedings of the 59th Hawaii International Conference on System Sciences (HICSS 59), 2026.

A map of what the field means by teacher AI literacy, built from 28 systematic reviews.

What we found
What we found
Theoretical foundations are fragmented, standardised assessment instruments are largely absent, and explainability receives little attention.
Why it matters
It sets the evidence base for a cohesive, ethics-aware framework, and shows why measurement is the field's most urgent gap.

Govern Published

AI literacy and governance in Indian education: mapping policy approaches and institutional initiatives

Shenoy, P. & Saarela, M. IEEE International Conference on Artificial Intelligence, Computer, Data Sciences and Applications (ACDSA 2026). DOI: 10.1109/ACDSA67686.2026.11467788

India's national AI literacy initiatives, benchmarked against UNESCO and OECD standards.

What we found
What we found
Policy framing and student competencies are strong; ethics and governance, teacher professional development and implementation are partial; data protection is the weakest dimension. High-risk deployments such as dropout prediction, biometric attendance and learning analytics run without adequate safeguards.
Why it matters
National ambition is outpacing governance, and teacher preparation is a bottleneck.

From post-hoc regulation to runtime governance: ethics and explainability in AI-driven education

Saarela, M. & Shenoy, P. IEEE 8th International Congress on Human-Computer Interaction, Optimization and Robotic Applications (ICHORA 2026). DOI: 10.1109/ICHORA69329.2026.11536988

Why AI regulation in education needs ethics built into the tool itself.

What we found
What we found
Post-hoc regulatory governance, as in the EU AI Act, and design-embedded ethical constraints work best as complementary layers, with explainability treated as a property of the interaction rather than a retrospective obligation.
Why it matters
Schools and vendors should ask not only "is this compliant?" but "does this tool explain itself to teachers when it matters?"

Contingent scaffolding in commercial AI tutors: establishing a baseline for agentic designs

Shenoy, P. & Saarela, M. IEEE ACDSA 2027, Rio de Janeiro, Brazil, February 2027.

Do AI tutors adapt their support to the learner the way good teachers do?

What we did
What we did
We probed four commercial AI tutoring modes with a scripted learner who struggles, gains competence and then regresses, comparing tutoring mode on and off and a purpose-built tutoring agent. Responses were blind double coded for directness.
Why it matters
It sets a baseline for judging future agentic tutors against a core principle of good teaching.
Build Accepted

Advancing AI literacy among educators in the Global South: evidence from a generative AI intervention in India

Shenoy, P. & Saarela, M. IEEE ACDSA 2027, Rio de Janeiro, Brazil, February 2027.

What a 90-minute, hands-on generative AI workshop does for teacher readiness.

What we did
What we did
44 teachers in Bengaluru used generative AI to design curriculum materials in a hands-on workshop. AI literacy readiness, measured with Technology Acceptance Model constructs, was higher after the workshop than before.
Why it matters
Short, practice-based sessions can move readiness in a large, AI-first education system.

AI credentials in computing education: a scoping review of curricular transformation, competencies, ethics, and evidence

Shenoy, P. & Saarela, M. ACM Transactions on Computing Education. Under review.

How universities are building AI credentials, and how mature that provision really is.

What we did
What we did
A PRISMA-ScR scoping review of 82 studies from 26 countries. Most programmes are still at proposal or pilot stage, few specify measurable learning outcomes, and many do not document how ethics is taught. We propose an AI Credential Maturity Model, levels L0 to L3, for institutional decision-making.

Human-AI division and collaboration in secondary L2 writing instruction: from classroom design to learning analytics

Deng, L. (lead author), Shenoy, P. & Saarela, M. IEEE International Conference on Education Technology and Computers (ICETC 2026), Porto, Portugal, December 2026.

The GenAI-TPD framework applied in a Chinese secondary classroom.

What the study did
What the study did
Led by Deng Lan, the third round of a two-year design-based research project in a junior high English-as-a-foreign-language classroom in Shenzhen, China, uses GenAI-TPD as its critical framework to investigate how teachers and AI divide the work of second-language writing, comparing students' original drafts with AI-revised versions.
Why it matters
It shows the framework travelling beyond its original contexts into a new subject, a new education system and a new research team.

In design Open to partner schools Field study, Indian schools

When AI suggests, do teachers defer?

AI grading assistants are arriving in schools. This study asks how much an AI-suggested grade pulls a teacher's own judgement, whether teachers can tell good AI advice from bad, and whether the same teachers also pass AI errors into the materials they use with students. It uses an established advice-taking design with an expert benchmark, so it can separate sensible trust from blind deference.

In progress With Dr. Mirka Saarela

Accountable pedagogical memory for AI tutors

As AI tutors begin to remember learners across sessions, what should they remember, and who can see or correct it? This journal paper designs and evaluates learner models for agentic AI tutors that are transparent, revisable and privacy-sensitive, rather than built on unrestricted persistence.

Proposed

LEARN: educationally aligned AI

Today's conversational AI is optimised to be maximally helpful, which can produce instant, satisfying answers, shallow understanding and dependence. LEARN proposes aligning educational AI to durable learning instead, through five mechanisms: calibrated guidance, retrieval, self-explanation, productive struggle and learner-centred interaction.

A study comparing human-facilitated and AI-facilitated debriefs in professional workshops, examining how AI changes reflective learning for adult learners.
